# CLAUDE.md

**Before any visual work (styles, layout, motifs, motion, 3D look, copy placement), read `docs/brand/design-direction.md`.** It sets the palette, typography, motifs, IP rules and the night cycle.

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project overview

Website for **Insomnia by Joel Soto**, a brand that merges two lines by Joel Soto:

- **Black & White by Joel Soto**: handmade steel jewelry (9 pieces).
- **Obscure Human Feelings**: a T-shirt line where each garment carries one word for an emotion that language has not named yet, with its meaning (summary in `docs/brand/obscure-human-feelings-summary.docx`).

The site keeps the Black & White aesthetic (minimalism, black and white palette, a 3D steel chain behind the home page) but is rebuilt in React + TypeScript and deployed on Render as a static site. Repo: `github.com/dajozu2513/Insomnia`.

Current state: the whole home page in the Insomnia aesthetic (intro splash, hero, pillars, three text-free bands, the jewelry deck and the printed T-shirts, each with its configurator and WhatsApp inquiry, and the Readings index), the analog header clock, the footer line, and the three article pages (`/lecturas/:slug`) with title-morph view transitions. The hero is the entrance and the fall (step 11): a tall, scroll-driven, first-person 3D sequence from the edge of a hole in the earth down a shaft that widens into mist for the rest of the page; the steel chain hangs only in band 1. Step 16.5 made the hole read as earth: irregular, ledged walls in tilted strata, dry and matte, lit only by the pale dusk through the mouth, with dust and grains of soil instead of glowing motes and the rose reduced to a small light far below. Step 12 filled the shaft with the objects of the fall (a falling piano, a DRINK ME bottle and an EAT ME cake whose words follow the language, porcelain, watches, cards, books, keys, roses), letters that spell a T-shirt word in band 2 and pages in band 3. Step 14 replaced the T-shirt grid with a floating clothing rack in the shaft (the grid remains the no-WebGL fallback). In the jewelry dialog, choosing a form turns the card over to a 3D preview of the piece (the link along the form, at the chosen length). Step 13 ends the page with the landing: the hall appears below upside down, turns over once and the visitor settles on its chessboard floor at dawn, facing a small door that is a "Fall again" button; the footer sits over its last frame. The 3D scene has its night atmosphere (in the misty shaft: rose light from below, motes, fog and lighting that follow the night cycle); it runs on the home page only.

## Stack

- Vite 8, React 19, TypeScript 6 in strict mode (plus `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`)
- react-router-dom 7 (`createBrowserRouter`)
- three `0.183.0` and `@types/three` `0.183.0`, pinned exactly: the legacy chain was tuned on this version, so do not upgrade it without comparing the look side by side
- Plain CSS with custom properties. No UI or CSS framework, no CSS-in-JS
- ESLint 10 (flat config, typescript-eslint strict type-checked, react-hooks, react-refresh) and Prettier
- npm, Node 24 (`.nvmrc`; Vite needs Node 20.19+ or 22.12+)

TypeScript is held at `~6.0` because typescript-eslint does not support TypeScript 7 yet.

## Styling

- Design tokens live on `:root` in `src/styles/tokens.css`, following `docs/brand/design-direction.md`:
  - Palette: `--ink`, `--ink-raised`, `--bone`, `--bone-deep`, `--text-on-dark`, `--text-on-light`, `--steel`, `--accent`, `--accent-glow`.
  - Derived (added, AA-checked): `--text-on-dark-muted`, `--text-on-light-muted` for secondary text; `--border` (alias of `--bone-deep`, 1px rules on light); `--bone-raised` (card paper); `--tee-white` (#f7f5f0, the white T-shirt, brighter than the paper so it reads as white on bone).
  - Channels for translucent overlays: `--ink-rgb`, `--text-on-dark-rgb`, `--steel-rgb`, used as `rgb(var(--ink-rgb) / 0.55)`.
  - Type: `--font-display` (Cormorant Garamond: titles, phrases), `--font-body` (EB Garamond: body text, set on `<body>`), `--font-ui` (Montserrat: labels, UI, clock; set it explicitly on every label); layout: `--gutter`, `--header-height`, `--radius`.
  - The legacy `--bg`, `--surface`, `--ink-mid` and `--ink-light` are gone (`--bone` and the muted text tokens replace them).
- Never hard-code a colour: use a token or a token channel. Light sections use `--bone` with `--text-on-light`; dark sections use `--ink` with `--text-on-dark`. Line art on light sections uses `--text-on-light` or `--text-on-light-muted`, never `--steel`. Red (`--accent`) is one focal point per screen at most, text only on light; on dark it is `--accent-glow` light, never text; and never in persistent UI (header, footer, language toggle).
- Every text pairing must pass WCAG AA. `/lab` measures them live from the tokens; check it after changing a colour.
- Atmosphere utilities in `src/styles/atmosphere.css`: `.atmo-grain` (film grain via `--grain-image`, also used by the home bands), `.atmo-vignette`, `.atmo-mist`. All stop moving under reduced motion.
- Fonts are self-hosted (`src/styles/fonts.css`, files in `src/assets/fonts/`, generated from the Fontsource packages): Cormorant Garamond 300/400/600 and italic 300; EB Garamond 400 and italic 400; Montserrat 200/400/500; latin and latin-ext subsets (`unicode-range`), woff2, `font-display: swap`. Vite hashes the files (long cache under `/assets/`). The intro and hero title face (Cormorant Garamond 300, latin) is preloaded by the `preloadTitleFont` plugin in `vite.config.ts`, which finds the hashed name in the bundle. Each family has a metric-matched fallback face (`… Fallback`: Georgia or Arial with `size-adjust` and ascent/descent overrides measured in the browser) in the token stacks, so the swap does not move the layout. To add a weight: copy its two woff2 files from the Fontsource package and add both `@font-face` rules (with their `unicode-range`). No Google Fonts requests.
- Ornaments are thin line art: use the motifs in `src/features/motifs/` (stroke `MOTIF_STROKE`, 1.3 px at any size, as the legacy pillar icons). One motif per section at most; objects and patterns only, never characters (see the IP rules in the design direction).
- Image paths are lowercase (`/images/fall/edge-wide.avif`). Render's file serving is case-sensitive. There are no product photos on the site: the links are drawn (`features/deck/linkArt.tsx`).

## Internationalization rules

- All user-facing copy is bilingual. Spanish is the default; English is the alternative.
- Copy lives only in `src/i18n/es.ts` and `src/i18n/en.ts`, never inline in components. Read it with `const { t } = useI18n()` and `t.section.key`.
- `es.ts` is the schema: `Dictionary` is `typeof es`, and `en.ts` is typed as `Dictionary`. A key missing in either file, or present in only one, is a compile error. **Always add every new key to both files.**
- The choice is saved in `localStorage['insomnia-lang']` (reads and writes are wrapped in try/catch) and mirrored to `<html lang>`.
- Strings in `[brackets]` are placeholders for copy Joel has not written yet, marked with a `// PLACEHOLDER` comment. Never invent brand copy; add a placeholder instead.
- Text that is the same in both languages (product names, the brand name) stays identical in both dictionaries or stays out of them. In the legacy site product names were never translated because they are lookup keys.
- No emojis anywhere: code, comments, docs or UI.

## Home page (`src/features/home/`, `intro/`, `night/`)

Section map and look come from `docs/brand/design-direction.md`; behaviour is ported from the legacy CLAUDE.md ("Home page"), `js/main.js` and `css/styles.css`.

- **Scroll frame** (`src/lib/scrollFrame.ts`): every scroll-driven effect subscribes with `subscribeScrollFrame(listener)`. One passive scroll/resize listener plus a body `ResizeObserver`, batched into one rAF; listeners write to the DOM through refs. Never add another window scroll listener for visual effects, and never set React state per frame (the header's `.scrolled` flag is the only state, and it only changes when it flips). `frame.progress` is page progress, or, while `setProgressSource(element)` is set (article pages set their text), the reading progress through that element (legacy article.js formula: 0 until its top passes 40% of the viewport, 1 when its bottom reaches the viewport bottom); the header hairline and the night clock both follow it. There is no smooth scrolling (`scroll-behavior` stays auto): route changes must jump before a view transition captures the new page.
- **Night cycle** (`features/night/night.ts`): night progress is the scroll frame's progress (0 at the top = 6:00 PM, 1 at the bottom = 6:00 AM; on article pages, the start and end of the text). `subscribeNightProgress(cb)` calls back inside the scroll frame; `getNightProgress()` returns the latest value. Step 4 drives the 3D lighting from `subscribeNightProgress`, without React.
- **Scroll clock** (`night/ScrollClock.tsx`): an analog `<PocketWatch>` at the top left of the header, in `.header-left` beside the brand block and as tall as it (`calc(clamp(1.1rem, 2.2vw, 1.5rem) + 0.6rem + 4px)`: about 38 px on desktop, 31 px on phones). Double rim, hidden (`.pocket-watch-inner-rim`) below 900 px where the watch gets too small for it. Behind `SHOW_SCROLL_CLOCK`. The hands are `<g>` elements whose `transform` is written through refs in the scroll frame (`watchAngles`: hour 180 + 360p, minute 4320p), continuously, so it also follows the scroll with reduced motion; they render without a transform prop so React never overwrites them. `?night=` freezes it. Colour is `currentColor` (the brand's colour), with a drop shadow over the hero. `aria-hidden`. On phones the tagline hides (below 600 px); the watch, brand and toggle fit down to 320 px, so the watch never hides.
- **Intro** (`intro/IntroSplash.tsx`): "INSOMNIA" rises with "by Joel Soto" under it (the shared by-line style), the hairline draws, the pocket watch fades in at 6:00 (static, double rim, 64 to 88 px, in `--steel` at 62%, the hairline's quiet level; fully in by 1.6 s, before the 2 s lift), then a clip-path curtain lifts after 2 s. Behind it, `.intro-red-mist`, the rose mist: two layers of eased radial gradients from `--accent-rgb` / `--accent-glow-rgb`, gathered in the bottom and the lower corners (the upper half and centre stay clean ink), blended with `screen` over soft cores of light (`--mist-light-rgb`) so it glows rather than lies flat, drifting slowly (static with reduced motion), under a lighter vignette and the grain; the design's one large use of red. The splash is opaque ink at z-index 999, so the chain canvas never shows through it. Once per tab (`sessionStorage['insomnia-intro-seen']`). Whether to show is read in a state initializer (pure) and the key is written in the effect, so StrictMode's double mount is harmless. `index.html` adds `intro-pending` to `<html>` before first paint on a first visit to `/` (page stays ink, no flash); while it is set the hero entrance is paused (`animation-play-state`). Skipped for a valid deep link, for an anchor (`/#lecturas`), and when arriving from another page of the site (location key other than `default`).
- **Hero** (`home/Hero.tsx`, `Hero.css`): the entrance. A tall section (`.has-3d .hero` is 280svh, only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; otherwise 100svh) with a sticky, full-viewport `.hero-pin` inside, holding `.hero-still` (the still of shot 1, `photo.css`; always set, it is the first paint and crossfades away in 2 s once `ChainStage` adds `chain-live` to `<html>`), `.hero-vignette` and `.hero-float` (the name, at 17% from the top, over a dark radial hush that keeps the text AA over the scene, measured at least 9.9:1 at the 99th percentile of the pixels behind it). The scroll frame reads `entranceProgress()` (`features/fall/fall.ts`) and fades and lifts the name over shot 2 (smoothstep 0.05 to 0.26). The h1 keeps the letter-by-letter title (`aria-label`, chars `aria-hidden`) and "by Joel Soto". The by-line is `.name-byline` (`home/byline.css`, shared with the intro): Cormorant Garamond italic, `clamp(1.45rem, 2.6vw, 2.1rem)`, chosen over Montserrat spaced capitals at the same size because it reads as part of the name. The former hero line is now `meta.description`: `index.html` ships the Spanish text for crawlers and `LanguageProvider` swaps it with the language (keep both in sync). No call to action. The home page stays ink until the app mounts (`home-route`, set in `index.html`; `global.css`), so the bone body never flashes before the still.
- **Pillars** (`home/Pillars.tsx`): Steel / Words / Vigil on bone, icons from `PillarIcons.tsx` (link, open quotation mark, crescent moon) that draw themselves: a ref measures every stroked shape into `--len` and adds `.draw`; shapes with `stroke="none"` get `.fill` and fade in after. A filigree divider sits above, in `--text-on-light-muted`.
- **Window mist** (`.atmo-mist`, bands): high veils, never a band along the bottom; positioned inside its window and never moved by scroll; its only motion is a slow, time-based horizontal drift (`mist-drift`), off with reduced motion.
- **Bands** (`home/Band.tsx`): three of them (after the pillars, after the jewelry, after the T-shirts); pure, text-free windows onto the misty shaft (`aria-hidden`), 460 px tall (300 px on phones): mist, a soft edge vignette and grain, nothing else. Only band 1 carries the chain (`<Band chain />`, `useChainBandRef(true)`), draped across its centre (see DRAPE_STYLE); band 2 shows the letters that spell a T-shirt word, band 3 the falling pages and open books (see "The objects of the fall"). Without WebGL they show the shaft still, which drifts and eases out of a 1.1x zoom (static with reduced motion; never `background-attachment: fixed`).
- **Jewelry deck** (`features/deck/`), inside the jewelry section (opaque bone, so the chain layering rule holds):
  - **Data:** `src/data/links.ts` (`LINKS`: id, name, rank, chainType, technical, poetic; localized fields are `Record<Lang, string>`, so both languages are required) and `src/data/forms.ts` (`FORMS`, `SIZES`). Link names are never translated and never get an i18n key. UI copy (labels, the WhatsApp template) lives in the dictionaries under `home.deck`.
  - **Add a link:** an entry in `LINKS` (id, name, rank, texts in both languages), a drawing in `linkArt.tsx` (64-unit box, `MotifSvg`, solids `stroke="none"`), and its id in `linkArtMap.ts`. **Add a form:** an entry in `FORMS` (`OTHER_FORM_ID` reveals the free-text field; `CUSTOM_SIZE_ID` the cm field).
  - **Deal:** the cards start face down and turn face up one by one (0.14 s apart) the first time the deck is 20% in view; face up from the start with reduced motion.
  - **Card red rule:** nothing on a card is red at rest; only the suit marks of the hovered (fine pointers), focused (`:focus-visible`) or selected card turn `--accent`; in the dialog, its card's marks are the one red. Hover lifts the card 6 px and dims the others to 0.7 (not lower: their text must stay AA).
  - **Dialog** (`LinkDialog.tsx` on the shared `product/ProductDialog.tsx`, ported from the legacy product modal): a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()` (top layer, `aria-modal`, focus moves in, the page is inert so focus stays inside), labelled by the link name. The card flies in from the grid with a 750 ms FLIP (skipped with reduced motion or when there is no on-screen source); overlay and panel animate only their background colour; closing fades out in 350 ms. Escape and a click on the backdrop close it; focus returns to the card; `html { overflow: hidden }` locks the page (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` keeps the layout from shifting). Choosing a form turns the card over to a 3D preview of the piece (see "The jewelry preview").
  - **WhatsApp** (`deck/whatsapp.ts`, on the shared `composeMessage`): `buildMessage(link, config, lang, strings)` composes the message (link, form, size; lines without a value are left out; "Otra (text)"; "A la medida (18 cm)" with the number localized); `product/whatsapp.ts` `whatsappUrl()` builds the wa.me link. It updates live and re-renders on language change.
  - **Deep links:** `?link=<id>` opens that card's dialog on load (and skips the intro); opening and closing keep the parameter in sync with `replaceState`; unknown ids are ignored.
- **Shared product dialog** (`features/product/`): both grids use `useProductDialog(param, find)` (open state from the deep link, `triggerRef(id)` on each button for focus return, `sourceRef(id)` on the element that flies) and render their dialog on `<ProductDialog>` (`className` modifier `product-dialog--link` / `--tee` sizes `.product-dialog-fly` in the product's CSS; `media`, optional `mediaFooter`, body as children). Configurator questions are `<ChipGroup>` (radio chips; arrow keys move within a group), the call to action is `<WhatsAppButton message>`, and every message is `composeMessage(intro, [[label, value]])`, which leaves out lines without a value. Shared copy is `home.product` (close, cta, newTab).
- **Deep-link parameters** (`product/deepLink.ts`): `?link=` and `?tee=`, in that priority order. On load the first valid one opens and the others are ignored (one dialog at a time); opening writes its parameter and removes the others; closing removes them all. `activeDeepLink()` also tells the intro to skip, and `index.html` skips `intro-pending` for either parameter. To add a product type, add its parameter and finder to `PARAMS` and the regex in `index.html`.
- **Printed T-shirts** (`features/tees/`): since step 14 they hang on the floating clothing rack (`features/rack/`, see "The clothing rack (step 14)" below); the grid below is the fallback without WebGL, inside an opaque bone section:
  - **Data:** `src/data/tees.ts`: `TEES` (id, word, meaning `Record<Lang, string>`, composition id), in order Sonder, Onism, Monachopsis, Nodus Tollens, Anemoia, Liberosis, Exulansis, Altschmerz, Zenosyne, Énouement; options `TEE_COLOURS`, `TEE_SIZES`, `PRINT_LANGUAGES` (each language named in itself, Español / English), `PLACEMENTS`, `TEE_SIDES`; `PREVIEW_DEFAULTS` (black, front and back); `printContent(placement, side)` ("full": word and meaning; "word": the word alone, the front of a front-and-back print; null). The word is never translated and never gets an i18n key; meanings are our own words.
  - **Add a design:** an entry in `TEES` (slug id: lowercase ASCII, hyphens, e.g. `nodus-tollens`, `enouement`), its id in `CompositionId`, a composition in `print/compositions.tsx` (wrap it in `<PrintFrame {...props}>{(region) => ...}</PrintFrame>` and draw the word inside `region`), and its entry in `print/compositionMap.ts` (typed, so a missing one fails to compile). It shows up in the grid, the dialog and `/lab`.
  - **Prints:** 200 x 240 print units; one colour (`currentColor`: ink on the white tee, bone on the black one); the word in Cormorant Garamond capitals laid out letter by letter from `metrics.ts` (advance widths measured from the loaded fonts; add a glyph there if a new word needs it), the meaning in EB Garamond wrapped into balanced lines, and the mark "INSOMNIA by Joel Soto" at the bottom of every print. Print-safe: flat tints only (0.05 steps, never below 0.2), strokes of at least 0.9 print units, no gradients; fades are made with knocked-out specks or lines (masks), never gradients. Random layouts (Sonder's crowd, Altschmerz's wear) use `seeded()`, so they never change.
  - **Mockup** (`TeeMockup.tsx`): a flat tee in line art (300 x 330), black (`--ink`) or white (`--tee-white`), seams only, the print at a realistic size: front on the chest (96 units wide; 108 for the word alone), back higher and wider (108). Front view shows the inside of the back collar.
  - **Grid:** 5 x 2, two columns at 860 px and below; black tees, front view, `PREVIEW_DEFAULTS`, shown as a close crop on the chest print (`teeGeometry.ts` `GRID_CROP`: x 74, y 14, 152 x 152 tee units, the same frame for all ten; the collar, shoulder line and armhole seams stay in frame so it reads as fabric; no drop shadow). The crop doubles the print's size: cap height of the longest words at grid size went from 4.8 / 5.5 / 4.8 px (Altschmerz / Zenosyne / Énouement, desktop) to 9.4 / 10.9 / 9.5 px, and from 3.9 / 4.5 / 3.9 to 7.6 / 8.8 / 7.7 px on a 390 px phone. The dialog keeps the whole tee at realistic print scale; its FLIP starts from the crop (`ProductDialog` `sourceCrop`, `GRID_CROP_FRACTION`): the whole tee is scaled so the crop lands exactly on the grid figure and clipped to it (`clip-path: inset()`), then opens out. The dialog hides horizontal overflow and reserves its scrollbar gutter, so the flying tee never makes a scrollbar appear and shift the panel. Reveal: the slots rise and fade in one by one (0.09 s apart) the first time the grid is 15% in view (opacity only with reduced motion). **Tee red rule:** nothing is red at rest; the hovered (fine pointers) or focused tee's short rule under its name turns `--accent`. Hover lifts 6 px and dims the others to 0.7, as the cards. Credit line under the grid (`home.tees.credit`, the title in `<cite>`).

Until chosen, the mockup previews black, front and back, with the meaning in the site language; choosing "Front" or "Back" turns the mockup to that side. The mockup's accessible name follows the view ("Camiseta Sonder, negra, vista de frente"); grid buttons read "Sonder, camiseta". Optional `sourceCrop` (the grid crop by default; null for the whole tee, the rack), `previewColour` (black by default; the rack tee's colour) and `previewPlacement` (front and back by default; `front` from the rack, so the flying mockup shows exactly the rack tee's print until the visitor chooses). ## 3D scene (`src/features/chain/`)

One fixed, full-viewport canvas (`z-index: -1`) behind the home page: the fall (step 11, below) and one long steel chain in band 1, ported from the legacy `js/chain3d.js` with the same geometry, studio lighting, material, tone mapping, exposure, pixel-ratio cap and motion constants. The look and every constant are documented in the legacy CLAUDE.md ("3D chain") and in `engine/constants.ts`; change a value only on purpose and record why.

### Architecture

- **Engine** (`engine/`, plain TypeScript, no React): `createChainScene(container, options)` creates its own `<canvas>` inside the stage, builds the renderer, studio environment and instanced chain, and returns `{ rebuild, setReducedMotion, setLabels, dispose }`. Modules: `constants.ts` (every tuned number), `geometry.ts` (stadium link, steel material), `studioEnvironment.ts` (the studio baked with PMREM), `buildPath.ts` (the chain's path from the live layout, pure), and the fall's modules (see The entrance and the fall). Scroll easing, pull speed, flow and the render loop live in closures and `setAnimationLoop`; **scrolling never touches React state**.
- **`<ChainStage />`** renders the fixed stage `<div>` and owns the engine lifecycle. It loads the engine with `import('./engine/createChainScene')` (`createChainScene-*.js`, 49 kB / 18 kB gzip since the hole became earth in step 16.5), and three.js lands in its own shared chunk (`three-*.js`, 563 kB / 143 kB gzip, also used by the jewelry preview; until the preview, three.js and the engine were one 597 kB chunk), so the main bundle stays light. Nothing outside `engine/` may import `three` at runtime (`import type` is fine); `types.ts` and `index.ts` must stay three-free.
- **StrictMode / remounts:** the import is guarded by a `cancelled` flag, so the double mount in development creates one engine. `dispose()` stops the loop and every listener, observer, timer, idle callback and pending rAF, disposes the link geometry, material, instanced mesh, the objects of the fall (every geometry, material and canvas texture; objects still being built are disposed when they arrive), environment render target and renderer, forces context loss and removes the canvas. It is safe to call twice.
- **Rebuilds:** on window `resize` and when `document.body` changes size (`ResizeObserver`, debounced 120 ms), because fonts and images move the bands; also when an anchor mounts or unmounts.
- **Loop:** runs only while the hero or a band is intersecting the viewport and the tab is visible. With reduced motion (from `usePrefersReducedMotion()`, live) there is no loop and no parallax (factor 1); the scene redraws on scroll only, and the hero holds shot 1 (normal height, no fall).
- **Fallback:** an inline script in `index.html` adds `has-3d` to `<html>` before first paint when WebGL and modules exist and the page is not on `file://`. The stills (`home/photo.css`, `--edge-still` and `--shaft-still`: AVIF, WebP, JPEG through `image-set()`, a tall crop at `max-aspect-ratio: 4/5`): the hero's is always set (first paint, preloaded in `index.html`); the bands' only under `html:not(.has-3d)`, so it is never downloaded while the scene runs. If the dynamic import or `WebGLRenderer` fails, or the stage throws (its `ErrorBoundary`), `has-3d` is removed and the stage unmounts: the hero drops to normal height and the stills stay.
- **Loading:** the engine is imported only after `document.fonts.ready`, the first-visit intro having lifted (`intro-pending` gone, then 700 ms for the hero title's letters to start painting; a 6 s safety) and an idle callback (`afterFirstPaint` in `ChainStage.tsx`, 800 ms timeout), so its start-up, the page's longest task, never lands on the intro or on the title's first paint (the largest contentful paint). Its shaders compile with `renderer.compileAsync` (KHR_parallel_shader_compile where the browser has it) before the first frame, and the objects' before they join the scene; nothing is drawn until then (`compiled`), and the shot 1 still covers the wait. In the launch review this took the blocking shader wait from about 2.4 s to 0.8 s under Lighthouse's 4x CPU slowdown, and the engine's start-up task from 3.8 to 1.8 s. The studio environment's PMREM bake (about 0.6 s on a laptop GPU, 0.8 s in software at 4x) runs in a task of its own after the renderer and the scene are built, so neither long task stacks on the other.
- **Context loss:** the engine listens for `webglcontextlost` (prevents the default, stops the loop, calls `options.onContextLost`). `ChainStage` disposes it and builds a new engine on a fresh canvas once the tab is visible again; after `MAX_CONTEXT_LOSSES` (3) in one visit it falls back to the stills. Test: `canvas.getContext('webgl2').getExtension('WEBGL_lose_context').loseContext()`.
- No pointer or device-orientation tracking, ever: the owner wants the motion calm and driven by scroll only.

### The entrance and the fall (step 11)

The look is recorded in `docs/brand/design-direction.md` ("The entrance and the fall"); the objects (step 12) and the landing (step 13) are recorded there too.

- **World:** the shaft's axis is vertical at x = 0, z = `SHAFT_Z` (the page camera's distance), the ground at y = 0. The camera only descends and pitches (rotation order YXZ): no yaw, no roll, no shake. After the hero it is the page camera, level, at `pageCameraY(scroll)`, so every band looks across the same shaft.
- **Sequence** (`entrance.ts`, pure): `entranceCamera(p, wide)` gives y, z, pitch and field of view for the entrance progress p (0..1): shot 1 at the rim (`EDGE_EYE`, `EDGE_STAND`, `EDGE_PITCH` -50), over the edge (0.1 to 0.3), the fall (accelerating, then steady, to `FALL_DEPTH`), the look up (`MOUTH_PITCH` around 0.52 to 0.74), level by 1 with the field of view easing from `HOLE_FOV_WIDE` / `_TALL` to `CAMERA_FOV`. `cameraForScroll()` picks the override (`?fall=`), the sequence, shot 1 held (no sequence: reduced motion or no tall hero) or the page camera. `shaftRadius(y)` widens the hole (`HOLE_RADIUS` 2.6) into the shaft (`SHAFT_RADIUS` 30) between `WIDEN_FROM` -46 and `WIDEN_TO` -78. `MathUtils.smoothstep` and GLSL `smoothstep` need edge0 < edge1: write a falling edge as `1 - smoothstep(lo, hi, x)` (a reversed call once inverted the shaft).
- **Edge** (`edge.ts`): the ground ring (`createGroundMaterial`: dry soil, crumbs and pebbles, a little moss, dark at the lip), 420 tufts of dry grass (`grassTuftGeometry`, `createGrassMaterial`; 220 on small screens) and 70 loose pebbles (40) as InstancedMeshes, five big roots (`taperedTube` through hand-placed points with `rough` 1.5: straight fibrous ridges and knots; four root hairs each where they dive in, `edgeRootHairs`), the pocket watch on the foreground root (in frame on phones too), the sky disc at the mouth (pale dusk, faces down, so it only shows from inside the hole: the light source seen in shot 4) and the deep glow sprite far below (`DEEP_GLOW_*`: small, dim, greyed rose; it lights nothing). Hidden once the camera is deep.
- **Pocket watch** (`pocketWatch.ts`): case and rim, crown, bow, an open hinged lid and seven small links, all in the chain's steel material (measured neutral) and merged into one mesh; a canvas dial (ticks only) darkened so it never glares, with mesh hands that show the night's hour (`setNight`, the header watch's angles); an additive glass cap whose glint sweeps across every 5.5 s (still with reduced motion, `dt` 0). Options make the floating watches (lid less open) and bare clock faces (no lid, crown or chain). `meshes` lists only the steel, for `measure(p, 'watch')`.
- **Shaft** (`shaft.ts`): `SEGMENTS_DESKTOP` 12 (9 small) open cylinder segments of `SEGMENT_HEIGHT` 5 (64 x 24 vertices, 44 x 14 on small screens: rows close enough for the ledges to break the wall in steps), recycled from above the camera to below it; per segment, instanced roots and half-buried stones (5 / 16, small 3 / 9), re-seeded from the segment index (`seeded`), so a depth always looks the same. Roots (`hangingRootGeometry`: a crooked main root with five root hairs and a split tip, merged) hang, jut out or push up at irregular angles. Both sit on the wall the visitor sees: `wallRadius()` (`wallShape.ts`) is the vertex shader's shape in TypeScript. None where the radius passes 3.8 (the wall dissolves there), none above the ground.
- **The hole reads as earth (step 16.5)** (`wallShape.ts`, `fallMaterials.ts`, `glsl.ts`). The look is in the design direction ("Shot 3, the fall").
  - **Shape** (`WALL_SHAPE_GLSL` / `wallRadius`, one formula in GLSL and TypeScript): an irregular cross-section that changes with depth, bumps, hard layers (`layerHash` above 0.62, masked by angle in `hardAt` so they are lenses, not rings) sticking out up to 0.45 as ledges and outcrops, crumbled hollows. The strata coordinate (`strataS`) is tilted (0.3 and 0.12 per unit of radius) and warped, with layers 1.35 apart on a distorted scale, so their thickness varies. In the hole the normal comes from the shape (two nearby points, finite differences in the vertex shader). Faded out at the mouth so the rim meets the ground.
  - **Table noise** (`tableNoise.ts`): every noise call of the earth (the shape, the walls' surface, bark, stones, ground, grass) is one fetch from a 256 x 256 seeded table (`createNoiseTexture`, one texture shared by the shaft's and the edge's materials, owned and disposed by the scene; `tnoise3` / `tfbm3` in GLSL and TypeScript). Hashed noise inlined at every call made the wall's vertex shader (the shape evaluated three times for its normal) take about 5 s longer to compile with ANGLE on D3D11: the scene went live at 10.7 s instead of 4.4 s in the production smoke test. With the table it goes live at 4.4 s, as before step 16.5, and the earth is cheaper to draw than the old walls. The shaft's bulge and `mistWall` keep the hashed `NOISE_GLSL`, so the bands are pixel for pixel the same.
  - **Walls:** dark soil, umber loam, muted ochre clay, pale clay and grey stone by layer; a dark topsoil band under the ground; fine grain, crumbs (noise specks), small pebbles, cracks mostly up and down and a seam at each layer's base, with bump mapping from derivatives (`bumped`); fine detail fades out with the pixel footprint. Matte: no specular, no sheen, no damp.
  - **Bark** (roots): dry and pale, fibres along the root (noise sampled on a circle around it, so no seam), furrows and split, flaking patches; no rim light. **Stones** and **ground** matte and speckled; **grass** dark at the root, pale olive grey at the tip.
  - **Light:** `holeLight()` is the cool, pale dusk (`SKY_LIGHT`) through the mouth, grazing down the walls (the dot with the direction to the mouth), times the mouth's aperture (1 / (1 + depth^2 / 81)), plus a dim bounce, a faint neutral light near the viewer and a floor. No rose in the hole.
  - **The shaft is unchanged:** where the walls have widened (`shaftness`, radius 7 to 20) they take the step 11 look (`mistWall`, `mistLight`: `MIST_SKY_LIGHT` and the rose from below), and only there does the fog turn rose (`fogTint`), so the hero's end and every band look exactly as before. The hole's walls dissolve into the shaft's mist between radius 3.8 and 14.
  - three.js passes `fogColor` already in the output colour space: use it as is.
  - **Measured** (`measure(0, 'walls')` / `'roots'`, motes off, dusk, desktop / phone): walls at `?fall=0.2` hue 26 / 27 degrees, HSV saturation 0.15 / 0.16, RGB 16/15/14; at 0.35 hue 29, 0.18, RGB 9/8/7; at 0.5 hue 32, 0.17, RGB 10/9/8 / 9/8/7. The lit wall pixels (brightest channel 24 or more) are neutral grey to umber (saturation 0.04 to 0.17, no pink). The pixels flagged pink (7 to 18%) are near-black (brightest channel under 16, `pinkByBrightness`), where a hue means nothing; from 16 up they are 0.4% at most. Before step 16.5: hue 6 to 9, saturation 0.39 to 0.57, 93 to 100% of the pixels pink. Roots hue 31 to 33, saturation 0.12 to 0.16 (before hue 3 to 9, 0.42 to 0.50, 97 to 100% pink). Watch steel at `?fall=0`, night 0 / 0.5 / 1: desktop 0.023 / 0.031 / 0.034, RGB 124/125/126; phone 0.024 / 0.031 / 0.034. The hero's name over the new shot 1: 12.7:1 (desktop) and 12.1:1 (phone) at the 99th percentile, by-line 12.5 and 11.6.
  - **Cost** (Iris Xe, offscreen, 240 frames, before / after, different sessions): desktop 1280x800 shot 1 8.9 / 7.5 ms, in the hole (`?fall=0.35`) 9.8 / 7.3; phone 390x844 6.7 / 5.2, 7.2 / 5.3. The fall's scene alone (everything minus `withoutFallScene`) went from 3.9 / 5.2 ms to 3.4 / 3.6 on desktop.
- **Small screens** (width up to `SMALL_SCREEN_MAX` 700): 9 segments instead of 12, 44 x 14 wall vertices instead of 64 x 24, 3 roots and 9 stones per segment instead of 5 and 16, 220 grass tufts and 40 pebbles at the edge instead of 420 and 70, 60 motes instead of 120.

### The objects of the fall (step 12)

The look and choreography are in `docs/brand/design-direction.md` ("The objects of the fall"). Modules in `engine/objects/`:

- **`fallObjects.ts`** builds and moves everything (`createFallObjects(steel, linkGeometry, small, data)`, `update(frame)` every draw). Objects are fixed in the world below the walls, so the descending camera passes them upward. Each kind sits on its own recycled lattice of heights (`KINDS`: spacing, phase, scale), like the wall segments: a cell's place, depth (9 to 25 from the camera; near ones rare), tumble and card face come from `seeded()`, so a depth always holds the same objects. Lattices start below `LATTICE_TOP` (-80); they keep ±`BAND_CLEAR` (4.5, what a band window shows at that depth) clear around bands 2 and 3, and around band 1 allow only keys and cards, far behind the chain. The piano is placed from the camera height (it falls more slowly than the visitor and turns, so everyone passes it in shot 5); the hero's end group (bottle, cake, tilted cup with drops of tea, watch, two cards) is hand placed around y -68 to -72, smaller on narrow screens. ### The landing (step 13)

The look is in `docs/brand/design-direction.md` ("The landing").

- **Camera** (`engine/landing.ts`, pure): the hall is built upright in room units (`ROOM`: 6 wide, 3.2 high, open at z +2, back wall at z -12) and placed turned half a turn about z (`roomOrigin`, `roomToWorld`: x and y negated), so in the world it hangs upside down below and ahead of the falling camera. `landingCamera(p)`: the camera keeps falling, easing out (`y0 - drop * (1 - (1 - p)^3)`; `landingDrop` makes its starting speed the page camera's), glides forward from the shaft's axis to the hall (smoothstep, starting from rest), rolls half a turn (`roll = PI * smoothstep(0.3, 0.7, p)`, the only roll in the site), pitches from -12 degrees (down at the room below) to `STAND.pitch` (down at the small door; up in world terms, the room being upside down) and widens its field of view to 52 degrees (74 on tall screens). At p = 1 it stands at `STAND` in the hall. `FallCamera` has `roll`; the engine sets `camera.rotation` (pitch, 0, roll) in YXZ order.
- **Progress:** `landingAt(scroll, map, land)` (entrance.ts): 0 when the landing's top reaches the top of the viewport, 1 at the bottom of the page (`LandingMap`: start, length to `scrollMax`, `y0 = pageCameraY(start)`, drop); before 0 the page camera falls on, so the hand-over is seamless. A landing with no sequence (reduced motion: the section is one screen high) holds p = 1 once it is halfway up the screen. `?land=` freezes it.
- **The hall** (`objects/hall.ts`, in the objects chunk, built at idle with them; reuses the kit's bottle, key and glass, and the floor module's `chessboardTexture`): floor, walls and ceiling (double sided, so from outside it reads as a closed box), skirting and picture rail; 13 doors as two InstancedMeshes (wood with four raised panels and a frame; steel handle), the small one scaled down in the back wall; the curtain (folded plane) on a steel rod; the line of light and a soft pool of it on the floor; three lamps (cord, porcelain shade with a warm emissive, a bulb and an additive halo); the glass table (`kit.makeTable()`), the key on it, the bottle beside it. The lamps' warm light and the garden's pale light are added in the hall materials' shaders (`onBeforeCompile`, positions in view space each frame), not as scene lights, so nothing else in the scene changes. Visible only near the landing (`y0 + 16`).
- **At the landing:** the shaft's walls are hidden near the hall (it reaches past their radius); the rose veils fade out by p 0.3, the glow to 15% by 0.6 (`glow.setStrength(night, fade)`), the motes to 25% (`particles.setOpacity`); no lattice object drifts below `y0 + 6`.
- **The door button** (`home/Landing.tsx`): on every rebuild the engine projects the small door at p = 1 for the current viewport and writes `--door-x`, `--door-y`, `--door-w`, `--door-h` (px) on the landing section; the button (at least 44 px) sits there in the sticky pin, which is the viewport for the whole landing. It shows past 0.94, stays in the tab order (focusing it scrolls to the bottom), and on click jumps to the top (`scrollTo`, behavior instant) and focuses `#main`. The engine takes a jump of more than 2.5 screens between two frames at once (`syncScroll`), so the camera does not ease through the whole page. Without WebGL the section sets the door from the stills (58.6% / 12.3 x 14.9 svh wide, 55.8% / 8.4 x 10.1 svh at max-aspect-ratio 4/5).
- **Small screens:** fewer curtain folds and lamp-shade segments; the kit's props as for the objects.

### Anchor API

Wrap the page in `<ChainAnchorsProvider>` and render `<ChainStage />` inside it. Windows report their elements with ref hooks, never through class-name queries:

```tsx
const heroRef = useChainHeroRef(); // <section ref={heroRef} className="hero">
const bandRef = useChainBandRef(); // <section ref={bandRef} className="separator">
const chainBandRef = useChainBandRef(true); // a band that carries the chain
const landingRef = useChainLandingRef(); // <section ref={landingRef} className="landing">
useChainRack(section, stage); // the T-shirt section and the stage the rack is centred on
```

- The registry (`anchors/registry.ts`) is plain mutable state outside React; registering never re-renders. Changes are batched into one `rebuild()` per microtask.
- Any number of bands can register (the home page has three); the chain's path is built only across the bands registered with `chain` (band 1), in document order, and is null when none is. After the last of them the chain rises away off screen on the other side, so no length of it trails into the windows below. Visibility (the render loop), night lighting and particles are not tied to a band count. Bands are sorted by document order. With `DRAPE_STYLE = 'centred'` (the default since the bands lost their text) every drape is a gentle catenary centred in its band, entering alternately from the left and the right; `'legacy'` restores the old low sag / high hang that cleared the band phrases.
- The engine reads positions on every rebuild: each band's document top and `offsetHeight`. The hero is used for visibility (render loop) and for the entrance: its document top and height map the scroll onto the camera (`ScrollMap` in `entrance.ts`). Each drape sits where the page camera will be when its band is centred on screen (`pageCameraY`).

### Night atmosphere (step 4; the rose atmosphere from step 10)

In step 10 the owner chose the "Rosa" atmosphere from three reviewed variants: the blood red, low dense mist and ash read as horror; the red is now the red of roses, the mist high, soft and luminous, and the particles tiny glowing motes. Its values are `--accent` / `--accent-glow` in `tokens.css` and the `MIST_*`, `GLOW_*` and `MOTE_*` constants in `constants.ts` (shader colours go through `rawColor.ts`: written as they show). The window mist (`.separator-mist`) sits in high veils, never a band along the bottom; it stays neutral warm white over the chain.

Modules in `engine/`: `particles.ts`, `glow.ts`, `heroMist.ts`, `nightCycle.ts`, plus `debug.ts` (development only); `floor.ts` (the chessboard floor; its `chessboardTexture` is the hall's floor at the landing). Tuned values live in `constants.ts` (ATMOSPHERE block) and `nightCycle.ts` (keyframes). **The chain must read as neutral silver steel at every hour; that wins over any atmosphere effect.** Never re-bake the PMREM environment per frame: it is what keeps the steel neutral.

- **Chessboard** (`floor.ts`): `chessboardTexture()` draws a 2 x 2 tile on a canvas (no image files), `FLOOR_DARK` / `FLOOR_LIGHT`, squares 1.8 world units, mipmapped with anisotropy so the far squares blur to an even grey: the hall's floor at the landing. The hero's chessboard floor plane left the scene in step 11; its code was removed in the launch review.
- **Motes** (`particles.ts`): one additive `Points` with a `ShaderMaterial` and a glowing `CanvasTexture` sprite (bright core, wide halo), `MOTE_COLOUR` warm white; 120 motes, 60 at widths up to 700 px. They rise at 0.09 units/s with a slow sway in a box that travels with the camera (z from -8 to 12, in front of and behind the chain); in the narrow hole they gather in a cylinder around the axis, from a little above the camera to well below it (blended by `holeBlend`); while falling fast they stretch into short vertical streaks (`uStretch`, from the camera speed, eased over 0.25 s, capped at `STREAK_MAX` 3.2). Their brightness breathes (each its own phase and rate, down to 40%), fading with the fog distance. In the hole (`uDust`, the same `holeBlend`) they are earth, not light (step 16.5): dust (`DUST_COLOUR`, `DUST_BRIGHTNESS`, a little over half of it showing, no halo, no breathing) hanging in the air and settling slowly, and one in four a grain of soil (`GRAIN_COLOUR`) that falls with the visitor, a little faster, and never streaks; in the shaft they are the glowing motes exactly as before. `draw(dt)` moves them and advances the breathing; with reduced motion the redraw passes `dt = 0`, so they stay still.
- **Rose veils** (`heroMist.ts`; since step 11 they belong to the open shaft, looking out across it: off in the hole and while the camera looks up or down): a camera-facing plane with three soft veils in the upper part of the frame (around 63%, 77% and 90% of its height), broken into long thin wisps by 3-octave value noise, with wide Gaussian pools of light (`MIST_LIGHT`) inside them as the glow (no bloom pass). Added as light (`CustomBlending` One/One) at `MIST_STRENGTH` 0.72 times their presence, drawn before the chain without depth test or writes, so every link is drawn on top: the steel is never tinted. Its slow drift is time-based and stops with reduced motion.
- **Glow:** an additive radial-falloff plane far behind the chain (z = -14) in `GLOW_COLOUR` (rose), following the camera and lifted towards the veils (`GLOW_LIFT`); strength `GLOW_BASE` + the night cycle's glow x `GLOW_GAIN` (0.05 at dusk and dawn, 0.33 at midnight). It adds light to the mist, never colour to the steel.
- **Night cycle** (`nightCycle.ts`): keyframes at 0 (dusk), 0.5 (midnight), 0.75 (small hours) and 1 (dawn), smoothstep-eased. Per keyframe: fog colour (also the canvas clear colour), fog near/far, tone-mapping exposure (1.13 to 1.15), rim light tint and intensity (a `DirectionalLight` behind and above), glow strength. The engine receives the night through `options.night` (`getNightProgress` / `subscribeNightProgress`, passed in by `ChainStage`), applies it only when the progress changes, and, when no loop is running (reduced motion), redraws from that same subscription, so the lighting follows the scroll on every redraw.

### Layering rule

The hero and the bands are windows onto the 3D scene: under `has-3d` they are transparent (the hero shows its still until the scene is live). **Every other home section must have an opaque background, or the chain shows through it. Never animate a section's own `opacity` (fade a child instead), or the black stage flashes through while it fades.** Keep the stage the only element with a negative `z-index`, and do not give ancestors of the home sections a `transform`, `filter` or `z-index` that would create a stacking context above the stage.

## Reduced-motion rule

Every animation must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The contract, inherited from the legacy site and documented at the bottom of `src/styles/global.css`:

- With reduced motion: no movement (no translate, scale or clip-path entrances, no parallax, no drift or looping, no scroll-driven camera, no smooth scroll). Short opacity fades may replace movement.
- The 3D chain must not run a continuous render loop; it redraws only on demand.
- In CSS, write the reduced variant in a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block next to the animation. In TypeScript, read `usePrefersReducedMotion()` from `src/hooks/` and skip the motion.
- Audited in the launch review (every CSS file and every JS animation): each animation and moving transition has a reduced branch or is an opacity or colour fade. By design, two things still follow the scroll with reduced motion, because they show where the reader is rather than move on their own: the header watch's hands and the progress hairline. In JS: the dialog FLIP is skipped; the page's 3D loop, the rack and the jewelry preview redraw on demand only.
